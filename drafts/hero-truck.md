# Hero Truck

*June 13, 2026*

Adventures in pet sitting.

Mari’s away. The girls are in for the night. We can still catch the tail end of JDM car night at Top Notch.

Boy, you in?

*Boy hops in truck.*

Sunset cruising to Dire Straits, Van Halen.

*Traffic slows on MoPac over Lady Bird Lake. Taking in the skyline at golden hour.*

A Ford F-150 is disabled in the center lane. No collision, no roadside assistance or officers on scene yet. I pull in front, make sure the boy is buckled in, put on the blinkers, and walk back.

Frustrated voices from the people being inconvenienced. Loud. Sounds like an alien language. I’m locked in.

Someone’s daughter. Someone’s mother. Both, actually, noticing the passenger. Both OK.

The tire is shredded. Her father and the police are on their way. I direct her to pull to the shoulder. I decide the potential wheel damage is worth getting them out of the center lane.

The truck has no spare. I’m fairly sure the Ford six-lug bolt pattern is the same as the Toyota’s. The police arrive. I explain my plan; they block the right lane to protect us.

I start lowering the Toyota’s spare. Her dad shows up and says he has one. He’s unfamiliar with the hoist—simple, but tricky in a pinch if you’ve never done it. He can’t lower his spare using my tools.

I collect my tools and go back to mine.

*Thank goodness we’d recently replaced the missing spare and defective hoist. Century Automotive on Jim Hogg Ave, Austin.*

## Jack

I lower my spare and stage it. Dad brings out a full-size hydraulic floor jack, places it under the axle, pumps. Doesn’t lift.

Release valve is probably open. I tell him to reinsert the handle and turn clockwise to close it. But the handle’s locking fastener is preventing it from seating.

Loosen the fastener. Handle seats. Pump. Saddle rises.

## Lug nut

All the mixed lug nuts come off except one.

Not a lock. Worse: stupid two-piece Ford lug nut.

Standard one-piece lug nuts are apex design. Inexpensive, standard, work equally well on an economy car or a performance car. Basic tools. Minimal failure modes. Last a lifetime.

Dunning-Kruger dimwits in Dearborn innovated a two-piece design with a cosmetic shell over the actual nut doing the mechanical work. When the shell fails, you get much the same effect as stripped threads. You’re left forcibly chiseling it away with improvised tools. Occasionally, a flesh wound.

Hostile design.

In a just world, the gods would sentence everyone in the corporate decision chain to Sisyphean roadside spare-tire duty in 100+ °F heat on the shoulder of IH-35 at rush hour while getting coal rolled.

I improvise a chisel.
