# Japan

*As a traveller, not a tourist. Field notes from November 2025.*

In my travel journal, I wrote:

> Japan has self-respect. Invests in its children, its future. Not an accident. Does the hard things. Pursues the impossible dream.

This is an impression I want to explore through the experiences behind it. What did I see that led me to write those words? What could I learn by paying closer attention?

## Thinking machines

November 10. Over Alaska, on the flight to Japan.

I was thinking about cars. Thinking machines. You drive; they help you think.

A note to myself: look at the S2000 videos from Mesa, July 1, 2024, where I’m talking to myself.

Don’t let the machines have all the fun.

## Room in the day

Slow mornings. The gym, pool, sauna. Reading the paper. Venturing out.

An enormous electronics store. An old underground mall. An unexpected Buddhist shrine. Strolling past vendors, then back to the Imperial Hotel and crashing hard.

Another day: Ginza. The Aston dealership at the Peninsula. A vegan bento box. The Nissan shop and a GT-R. A-PIT Super Autobacs. Later, picking up my luggage and moving to Shibuya.

I made it to Masaka, the vegan izakaya, on Friday. DEE DEE, a vintage / hippie store. Around Yoyogi Park: golden ginkgo trees, photographs, and an original NSX heard and seen.

There was Shinjuku Golden Gai and DJ Bar Bridge. A Scottish guy had strong opinions about music. I asked who he liked.

Jeff Mills.

Later, a change of pace: a stay in an amazing ’70s home. A lovely 7-Eleven dinner. Queen music videos.

## Quality in everyday things

The trains here. Especially in the smaller towns and more rural areas. I like a good train system. Full-spectrum, high-quality, efficient mobility.

Restaurant window displays that show you exactly what the food looks like. Wet towelettes. Subtle fragrances.

Small things that made it into the journal. What standards and habits make that kind of care ordinary?

## Following the car thread

At Bingo Sports in Japan, I met [Anna](https://www.instagram.com/annafeneru). We talked about our love for the LFA, events in Japan, and transporting cars around the world.

We’ve stayed in touch a bit since.

The car interests were part of how I found my way around: Nissan, A-PIT, Bingo. The planning notes also had driving in Hakone, museums, tuning shops, and car meets. There was much more on that list than I’ve recorded doing.

## Craft, wear & care

Another reference I saved: [Daniel Arsham’s 356 Bonsai](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/scene-passion/porsche-356-bonsai-daniel-arsham-artist-29720.html).

A 1955 Porsche Speedster, reworked with wabi-sabi in mind. Exposed welds, pitting, and wear. Traditional Japanese textiles. A car restored to be driven, with its age still visible.

A thread to return to: caring for something while allowing its history to remain present.
