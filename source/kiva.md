# Kiva: keeping money circulating

Many years ago, I learned about [Kiva](https://www.kiva.org/) and encouraged my work colleagues to contribute.

I remembered putting about $5,000 into it over a period of months, keeping repayments circulating through further loans, and then leaving it alone for years.

During this sabbatical, I went back to look at the record.

## What the record shows

My Kiva account overview, captured on October 30, 2026, reports:

| Measure | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Total deposits | $5,978.82 |
| Total amount lent, including relending | $51,490.00 |
| Loans made | 1,992 |
| Countries supported | 70 |

The cumulative amount lent is approximately **8.6 times the total deposits**. Repayments made it possible to lend the money again.

That is cumulative lending, not investment growth or a financial return. It also isn't a count of unique people or proof of a particular improvement in their lives. The record documents participation and circulation; understanding the outcomes requires looking more closely.

[My public Kiva lender profile](https://www.kiva.org/lender/ojas)

## What interests me

An initial commitment became a pool of money that could be used repeatedly. Returning to it years later gives me something concrete to examine alongside the [hypothesis](hypothesis.md) I'm exploring: doing good and thriving, and patterns that support healthy circulation in the economy.

## Still to investigate

- Confirm when I started and the timing of the deposits.
- Reconstruct how relending continued, including any automatic settings.
- Read individual borrower stories and repayment histories.
- Review defaults, currency losses, withdrawals, and other transactions before drawing conclusions about the remaining capital.
- Look back at the colleagues I encouraged to participate and any record of those invitations.
- Ask what the lending history can—and cannot—tell us about borrowers' outcomes.
