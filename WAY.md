# The way

## Purpose

This site is Ojas Patel’s inquiry into how to live, informed by a practice of taking regular breaks from an engaging career.

Its central statement is:

> I’ve made a practice of stepping away from an engaging career to examine how I’m living. This is a record of what I questioned, what I tried, what I noticed, and what I’m choosing next.

The work includes career, family, craft, maintenance, creativity, community, ahimsa, and questions whose significance is still emerging. Include material for what it reveals about the inquiry.

## Posture

Move between deliberate experiments and open exploration. Give experiments enough structure to learn from them; give experiences room to change the questions.

Do not require every experience to test a hypothesis or produce a measurable result. Curiosity, rest, relationships, and unresolved questions belong here.

## Evidence and precision

Distinguish recollection, observation, hypothesis, experiment, and conclusion. An inventory of activity is not automatically evidence of impact.

Preserve uncertainty. Keep approximate amounts and dates approximate until verified. Do not invent biographical details, motives, causal explanations, quotations, or outcomes to make the narrative smoother.

For an experiment, useful questions include: What am I trying? What would I notice? What happened? What changed my mind? Apply these only where they help.

## Voice and editorial care

Write in Ojas’s voice: specific, candid, curious, and direct. Prefer concrete experiences to abstract claims. Preserve roughness when it carries meaning, and identify working drafts as such.

Make light edits for readability without silently changing meaning. Preserve supplied quotations and correspondence; distinguish editorial framing from the original material.

Respect Ojas’s explicit editorial choices, including his decision to retain source paths and working links. Do not remove them merely because they are local or require account access. Never add credentials or secrets.

## Structure

Chapters carry the progression of the inquiry. Themes connect material across chapters. Pages can be reflections, field notes, experiments, retrospectives, or hypotheses without forcing those forms into a rigid taxonomy.

The working progression is:

1. Making room.
2. Opening the question.
3. Living the inquiry.
4. Taking stock.
5. What I’ll test next.

This is an editorial guide, not a requirement to create empty sections or rename existing pages. Let the structure evolve with the material. Keep sidebar order and Previous/Next navigation coherent, and preserve existing URLs where practical.

## Working practice

Make focused, reviewable pull requests. Read the current source and relevant PR comments before editing. Preserve unrelated changes and original source references.

### Drafts and refinement

Use checked-in Markdown as the canonical draft. Follow [drafts/README.md](drafts/README.md) and its small index to resume work.

New unpublished pieces live in `drafts/`, outside Sphinx’s `source/`. Refine the same file through focused commits or PRs; update its next move when useful. Keep source comparisons and editorial notes separate from the readable prose. Use issues for tasks and questions when helpful, not as a competing copy of the draft.

Publish by moving the same file into `source/` and adding it to navigation in a publication PR. For an already published page, edit its existing file on a branch until ready to publish. A page under `source/` is not hidden simply because it is absent from the navigation or labeled as a draft.

The repository is public. Draft storage prevents website publication, not GitHub visibility. Keep private source material in the private collection unless approved for the repository.

Use the existing Sphinx/MyST and Furo setup. Retain navigation, search, responsive layout, and appearance controls when editing visual styles. Validate changes in proportion to their risk and report what was actually checked.

These orientation files are internal repository guidance, not site content.

# Not the way

- A productivity scorecard that requires every hour off to justify itself.
- A polished personal-brand narrative that removes ambiguity, difficulty, or contradiction.
- A universal prescription for how other people should live.
- A predetermined argument that every experience must support.
- Treating activity, money circulated, or output volume as proven impact.
- Turning rough notes into confident claims or invented personal history.
- Premature taxonomy that creates more structure than the material needs.
